\section{Summary}
\label{sec:conclusion-summary}

This thesis presented a method for training a Sudoku solver
from examples of puzzle--solution pairs, supporting both
symbolic and visual input modalities. The predictor couples a
neural backbone --- a linear baseline for symbolic inputs,
LeNet-5 for visual inputs --- with a Markov Network decision
layer that scores complete labellings under the Sudoku
constraint graph. Training uses the LP-relaxed max-margin
(LP-M\textsuperscript{3}N) loss of
Franc \& Yermakov~\cite{franc2021}, which avoids any inference
oracle at training time by introducing per-example dual
variables. Inference at evaluation time uses Viterbi on chain
graphs and loopy max-sum Belief Propagation on the Sudoku
graph. The pipeline is implemented in the \texttt{mnlearn}
Python library, with every experimental decision driven by a
single YAML artifact that round-trips through the loader, and
is exercised on a benchmark covering two tasks (Sudoku, HMC)
and two input modalities.
