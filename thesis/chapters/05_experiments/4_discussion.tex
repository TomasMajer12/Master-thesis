\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:experiments-discussion}

\subsection{Why joint structured training beats the OCR baseline}
\label{subsec:discussion-joint-vs-ocr}

The two-stage OCR + solver baseline saturates near per-clue
accuracy on the LeNet-5 backbone: at $N = 5000$ the per-clue
error is below $0.009$, essentially the noise floor of MNIST
recognition. The puzzle-level $0/1$ error nevertheless plateaus
around $0.25$. The bottleneck is structural rather than
perceptual: with $\sim 36$ clue cells per puzzle, even a
sub-$1\%$ per-clue error rate produces a mis-read in roughly
one puzzle in three, and a single mis-read clue can render the
entire puzzle inconsistent with the all-different constraints.
The hard-coded solver returns ``infeasible'' on those puzzles,
which the evaluation counts as $0/1$ errors. The $\sim 75\%$
solver-feasibility ceiling in Table~\ref{tab:ocr-baseline} is
the empirical signature of this cascade.

The M\textsuperscript{3}N predictor breaks the ceiling for two
reasons. First, the unary potentials retain calibrated
uncertainty: an ambiguous handwritten digit emerges from the
backbone as a soft distribution over candidate labels, not a
hard pick. Second, the pairwise term lets that uncertainty
propagate through the constraint graph --- a clue cell whose
top-1 OCR prediction would violate a row or column can be
overridden once Belief Propagation has aggregated evidence from
the neighbors. The $5\%$ puzzle-level error at $N = 5000$ is
the joint payoff of these two mechanisms --- not better
perception, but perception that knows when to defer to
structure.

\subsection{Training asymmetry between backbone and pairwise}
\label{subsec:discussion-lr-pairwise}

A practical lesson from the Sudoku runs is that the backbone
and the pairwise matrix cannot share a learning rate. The
LeNet-5 backbone has roughly $60{,}000$ trainable parameters
and sees a dense gradient signal from every clue image in every
mini-batch; the pairwise matrix has $K^{2} = 81$ entries and
accumulates gradient only through the LP-M\textsuperscript{3}N's
pairwise sufficient statistics, which the backbone is
simultaneously trying to dominate. With identical learning
rates the backbone converges within tens of epochs while $W$
lingers near its small-Gaussian initialization, and the
predictor degenerates to a per-cell classifier whose soft
Sudoku prior is too weak to override mis-read clues. Setting
\texttt{lr\_pairwise} to ten times \texttt{lr\_model} re-balances
the optimization: $W$ acquires the diagonal-dominant
all-different shape within the first hundred epochs, and the
joint predictor escapes the per-cell-classifier plateau that
fixed-LR runs settle into.

\subsection{The softmax tail and $\phi$-saturation}
\label{subsec:discussion-phi-saturation}

The LeNet-5 backbone terminates in a softmax over the $K$
unary logits. The choice is empirically motivated: with the
softmax tail removed, the test $0/1$ error roughly doubles
(Figure~\ref{fig:phi-saturation}).

The mechanism is that the per-example duals $\phi^{i}$ absorb
unary magnitudes through the $U$ functional of
Section~\ref{subsec:lp-m3n}. Without an upper bound on the
unaries, $\|\phi^{i}\|$ inflates during training, the pairwise
term develops a weaker all-different shape, and the validation
$0/1$ stalls early. The softmax bounds the unaries in
$[\log(1/K), 0]$, keeps the duals finite, and lets the
constraint structure emerge from gradient pressure on $W$
rather than being out-competed by an inflating unary.

\subsection{Failure modes}
\label{subsec:discussion-failure-modes}

The dominant caveat in the visual-Sudoku results is that Belief
Propagation is exact only on trees: on the cyclic Sudoku graph
it can converge to a fixed point that is not the global MAP,
and occasionally returns a labeling that violates a Sudoku
constraint. The residual $\sim 5\%$ errors at $N = 5000$ have
not been broken down into perception-induced versus
decoding-induced failures; quantifying the BP gap against an
exact MAP decoder (e.g.\ an integer program over the constraint
graph) is left as future work
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-future}). That the same constraint
graph and decoder reach zero error on \emph{symbolic} Sudoku
(Section~\ref{subsec:experiments-sudoku-symbolic}) indicates BP
is not the rate-limiting factor when the unaries are clean ---
on visual data the breakdown is open.

\subsection{Limitations and threats to validity}
\label{subsec:discussion-limitations}

The empirical results should be read with the following
caveats. The Sudoku benchmark draws from a single source
(Kaggle's \texttt{bryanpark/sudoku}~\cite{park2018sudoku}) that
is essentially a single difficulty grade; generalization to
harder puzzles with fewer clues is not tested. The visual
variants are rendered from MNIST only; transfer to other
handwriting domains is open. The training-set sizes are limited
to $N \leq 5000$; behavior at much larger $N$ (the source has
$\sim 10^{6}$ puzzles available) is unexplored. The evaluation
decoder is Belief Propagation, which is approximate on the
Sudoku graph; the gap to an exact decoder is unmeasured
(Section~\ref{subsec:discussion-failure-modes}). Finally,
absolute headline numbers may shift with longer training
budgets, larger backbones, or alternative pairwise
parameterizations

\subsection{Connections to the broader literature}
\label{subsec:discussion-connections}

The LP-M\textsuperscript{3}N training path is due to
Franc \& Yermakov~\cite{franc2021}, who introduced the
relaxation as a tractable surrogate for max-margin learning
under missing labels; the consistency analysis was extended by
Franc, Pr\r{u}\v{s}a \& Yermakov~\cite{franc2022}. This thesis
applies the relaxation in the fully-labeled setting and uses
it as the principal training loss on cyclic graphs, where exact
hinge is intractable. The neuro-symbolic combination of a
learned perceptual backbone with a structured decision layer
connects to SATNet~\cite{wang2019satnet}, which uses a
differentiable MAXSAT layer in place of a Markov Network. The
two designs are conceptually close --- both isolate perception
from reasoning while training end-to-end --- and a direct
empirical comparison on a shared benchmark is a natural
extension of the present work
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-future}).
